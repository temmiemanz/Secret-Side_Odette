Lore
Odette was never found — she was delivered, or so she was told. Abandoned as a baby, the cult took her in and decided immediately that God had sent her to them. Her ability made the story easy to sell. She was raised quiet and obedient, trained to serve, and eventually made their priestess. She has killed people in that role. More than she can make peace with.
The cult wears black to mourn the world they believe is dying. Their highest honor is death — a public ritual killing that means you've earned heaven. The leaders haven't died yet because they claim it's their sacred duty to send others first. Odette believed all of it. Then she started pulling on threads.
What she found was that her mother had tried to leave when Odette was born — and they killed her for it. The story of the abandoned baby was a cover. The whole system was a lie built to keep people compliant and the powerful comfortable. She ran. She tells herself she was a coward for not exposing it. She's probably right, and she knows it.
Now she dresses in as much color as she can find — a deliberate contrast to the black she wore her whole life, and a practical disguise. She is relentlessly optimistic in a way that reads almost wrong given everything, but it comes from fear: she has no idea what happens after death, and that terrifies her more than anything the apocalypse can throw at her. Life feels like a gift because she doesn't know what comes next — and somewhere underneath that, she's still afraid she'll find out she was wrong to leave.
